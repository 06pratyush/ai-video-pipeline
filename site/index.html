A text script becomes a finished video

AI Video Studio is a free, open-source local AI video generator for Windows, Linux and macOS. Paste a script, pick a style, and eight stages later you have a narrated, scored, word-synced-captioned, colour-graded MP4 — rendered entirely on your own GPU. No cloud, no subscription, and nothing ever leaves your machine.

How it works

Eight stages, all running locally

When you press Generate, the backend queues your project and a worker thread walks it through the stages below. Each stage hands its output to the next, and every model stage runs on CUDA. Optional stages fail soft — if ACE-Step is missing you get a video without music rather than an error. The timings are measured on an RTX 4060 Laptop (8 GB VRAM, 16 GB RAM), which is the machine v2 was built and verified on.

  1. 1

    Narration & scene prompts Always

    Your raw script is rewritten into clean spoken narration — contractions, pacing, and the awkward phrasing that reads fine but sounds wrong — and then split into scenes, each with a visual prompt written for a diffusion model.

    Gemma 3 / 4B via Ollama · ~10s each

  2. 2

    Voice synthesis Always

    The narration is spoken by one of eight voices, on the GPU. Your own voice can be cloned from a reference clip through XTTS v2.

    Kokoro TTS · ~5s per 100 words

  3. 3

    Character sheets & keyframes Always

    This is what makes a cast survive a whole film. Each character is drawn once as a reference sheet, and then every scene’s first frame is drawn with that character in it — so the person in scene 20 is still the person from scene 1.

    FLUX.1 schnell (under a minute each) or FLUX.1 Kontext dev (3–8 min each, most consistent) via ComfyUI

  4. 4

    Video clips Always Slowest stage

    Each keyframe is animated into a clip on your GPU — image-to-video, so the motion starts from a frame you have already seen rather than from the text alone. 121 frames at 480×832, 20 steps.

    Wan2.2 TI2V-5B GGUF via ComfyUI · ~6 min per scene

  5. 5

    Frame smoothing Default on

    Every clip is interpolated on the GPU from 24 to 48 fps, per clip rather than across the joined film, so a cut never has a frame invented across it. Upscaling is a separate, optional pass.

    RIFE 4.9 · 12–39s per clip · optional Real-ESRGAN x2plus ~7 min per scene

  6. 6

    Background music Optional

    A score is composed on the GPU to match the mood your Skill specifies, then side-chain ducked beneath the narration and mixed to −14 LUFS.

    ACE-Step v1 3.5B · 34–40s per 80s of audio

  7. 7

    Word-synced captions Optional

    The rendered audio is transcribed back with a timestamp for every single word, and the script’s own words are burned in with the active word highlighted — which is why Social Short turns this on by default.

    faster-whisper small.en, CUDA float16 · seconds for a whole film

  8. 8

    Colour grade & mux Always

    A colour grade and optional film grain are applied, clips are joined with dissolves that land on each new line, audio is mixed, and the result is encoded to final.mp4 on NVENC.

    FFmpeg curves / eq / concat · ~50s

What makes it survivable

  • One heavy model at a time. The daemon unloads the LLM from Ollama before the video stage and checks free VRAM against what the stage needs, warning you up front instead of dying halfway through.
  • A persistent queue. Progress lives in SQLite, so closing the app mid-render does not lose the job.
  • A scene cache. Keyed on sha256(prompt + seed + model + resolution) — re-rendering a project only redoes the scenes that changed.
  • Soft failure. Optional stages degrade rather than aborting the render.

Architecture

Three tiers, each replaceable

The desktop app never talks to a model directly. It talks to a local daemon, and the daemon owns the model lifecycle.

Electron frontend

React 18 · Vite · Zustand · Tailwind

Project library, script editor, render panel, live VRAM meter, template and model browsers, batch modal.

REST + WebSocket 127.0.0.1:7860

Python daemon

FastAPI · SQLAlchemy 2.0 · uvicorn

Generation queue, VRAM-aware model lifecycle, scene cache, 11 REST routes and a WebSocket progress stream.

subprocess lazy start

Local runtimes

ComfyUI · Ollama · FFmpeg

ComfyUI runs FLUX.1, Wan2.2, RIFE and Real-ESRGAN; Ollama serves Gemma; FFmpeg encodes on NVENC. Started on demand, and stopped only if this app was the one that started them.

The daemon binds to 127.0.0.1 only and has no authentication, by design — the trust boundary is your machine. See the security policy for the full threat model.

Skills

One preset decides the whole look

A Skill bundles voice, speed, scene pacing, aspect ratio, music mood and the FFmpeg post-processing chain into a single choice. Seven ship by default, and they are plain JSON — copy one to make your own.

Setup runs once, then you can disconnect

The first launch installs Python and a venv, PyTorch and CUDA, ComfyUI and its dependencies, and pulls the Ollama model — about 17 minutes of work. On top of that sits the model stack itself, roughly 34 GB, so how long the download takes is down to your connection. scripts/setup_models.py fetches it resumably and checks every file against an exact byte size before keeping it. After that, nothing needs the network.

NVIDIA only, and verified on one machine

A full end-to-end render is verified on Windows on an RTX 4060 Laptop with 8 GB of VRAM. Linux, macOS and GPUs below 8 GB are not yet verified. AMD is not supported because the GGUF video path has no tested ROCm build, and CPU-only rendering is not practical for video — the honest status of each is tracked in CONTEXT.md.

FAQ

Common questions

Is AI Video Studio free?

Yes. It is MIT licensed and free permanently. There is no account, no metering, no paid tier and no telemetry. The only costs are your electricity and disk space.

Does it work offline?

Yes, after setup. The first launch installs the runtime and downloads the model stack, about 34 GB, and needs a connection. After that every render runs entirely on your machine and you can stay disconnected permanently. The only outbound request the app makes is an optional update check against GitHub Releases.

Do I need an NVIDIA GPU?

Yes. 8 GB VRAM is the minimum and a laptop GPU is enough, because the video model is a 4-bit GGUF build; 12 GB or more is recommended. CPU-only mode is not practical for video in v2. AMD GPUs are not supported yet, because the GGUF video path has no tested ROCm build.

How long does one video take?

Longer than a hosted service, because the video model runs on your own card. Measured on an RTX 4060 Laptop with 8 GB of VRAM: about 16 minutes for a 2-scene project through the render queue, and about 2 h 35 min for a 13-scene, 77-second film. Animating the clips is most of that, at roughly 6 minutes a scene; smoothing adds 12–39s per clip, music 34–40s for 80s of audio, and captions seconds for the whole film. Optional Real-ESRGAN upscaling is the expensive extra, at about 7 minutes a scene.

How is this different from Runway, Pika or Sora?

Those are hosted services: you submit a prompt, their GPUs render it, and you pay per generation under their content policy. AI Video Studio runs the same class of pipeline on hardware you own — unlimited renders, no upload, no content review, no recurring cost — in exchange for needing a capable GPU and the patience for a local render.

Can I use the output commercially?

The application is MIT licensed, so the code is yours to use. Output rights depend on the models you run — Wan2.2, FLUX.1, ACE-Step, RIFE, Real-ESRGAN, Gemma, Kokoro and Whisper each carry their own licence. Most of the stack is Apache-2.0, MIT or BSD-3, but FLUX.1 Kontext dev is non-commercial: pick FLUX.1 schnell for anything you intend to monetise. Check each model's terms against your intended use; this project cannot grant rights it does not hold.

Where are my videos stored?

Under app/projects/<project-id>/ beside the application, indexed by a SQLite database. Nothing is uploaded. Note that deleting a project removes its database rows but leaves the media on disk.

Can I add my own voice or visual style?

Yes. Voices clone from a reference clip through XTTS v2. Visual styles are Skills — plain JSON files you can copy and edit. See Skills.
